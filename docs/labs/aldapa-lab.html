Aldapeko Sagarra

A sketch for another game: a golden late afternoon just before autumn. On the highest branch of the old apple tree the txantxangorri sings the notes you play. Down the slope the baserri's lights come and go and its chimney smokes; the latxa sheep graze. And who will dance to that little tune? The sheep do.

MODE
Easy: any key
SOUND
txistu (flute), an octave up
MUSIC
soft chords on
Play: tap the keys, the computer keys A S D F G H J K L (white) and W E T Y U O P (black), or a MIDI keyboard where the browser allows it. Easy: any key plays the next note. Key: the next note's letter floats over the robin, and its key glows; a wrong key drops an apple on a sheep's head, and two in a row make that sheep poop while the robin hides its eyes. It always waits for you. K2 changes the sound: a txistu-like flute, a music box or the bird's whistle, each an octave above the keys you press.
The song is the traditional Aldapeko sagarraren (herrikoa), the melody only, as in the score: D minor, both halves repeated, 102 notes from A3 to D5. The chords under it are our own.
The look: our own pixel drawing, composed like a photograph: the sun setting on the left third, the robin on the upper-right third, sunset clouds lit from below, a misty valley, the baserri set into the hill, a dry-stone wall leading up to the tree, two latxa sheep with long shadows, soft grass across the bottom. A sketch to talk about, not the firmware yet.
