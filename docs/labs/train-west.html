Wild West Night Train

Old Number 7 steams across the desert at night, under a huge moon: a big engine with a balloon stack, a bell and a lamp, the tender full of logs and a puppy riding on top. Each note is a chuff. Play well and it turns into a golden super train.

MODE
Easy: any key
VOICE
harmonica
SONG
Working on the Railroad
MUSIC
boom-chick on
Play: tap the keys, the computer keys A S D F G H J K L (white) and W E T Y U O P (black), or a MIDI keyboard where the browser allows it. Each note is a chuff: the wheels turn, the engine moves on, a puff in the note's colour goes up. At the end of each line the whistle goes choo choo, the bell rings and the coyote howls along. Stop and the engine waits, hissing.
Mistakes (Key mode): one wrong key and the engine coughs black smoke. Two in a row and the engine itself sneezes: ah… ah… CHOO! Its front rears up, it slams down, the driver's cap flies off and the sooty cloud blows back over the train. The puppy barks WOOF!, and the driver goes red, frowns, shakes his fist out of the window and steams from the ears: HMPH!
Super train: eight right notes in a row in Key mode, or the end of the song in either mode. The engine turns gold, its smoke turns to rainbow stars, sparks fly from the wheels, and it races off with a whistle fanfare. A cowboy gallops alongside, waving his hat.
The song's end: a golden run with fireworks over the mesas, then the train pulls into Star Station. The sheriff, a lady with a lantern and a boy wave and cheer, and the sheriff throws the train a star. It keeps its stars on the tender, one more each time the song is played to the end. The next key sets off again.
Along the line: telegraph poles with an owl that hoots as you pass, a ranch with its windmill turning, a covered wagon camp with a campfire, a cowboy playing guitar and his horse, a longhorn that moos in the lamplight, prairie dogs that pop up as the train comes, a tumbleweed, bats across the moon, shooting stars. Now and then a canyon, crossed on a wooden trestle, the wheels clattering. A jackrabbit races the train, until it sits down, puffing. From "Dinah, won't you blow" on, the cacti bloom in the lamplight.
The songs (K3): the traditional I've Been Working on the Railroad (printed 1894, public domain), the verse and "Dinah, won't you blow", in G as the Digital Tradition has it: 93 notes, D4 to C5; and Funiculì, funiculà (Luigi Denza, 1880, public domain; the song of Vesuvius's funicular railway), its verse and its chorus, in G as an old piffero part and The Session have them: 64 notes, G4 to A5. K2 picks the voice: harmonica, banjo or steam organ.
