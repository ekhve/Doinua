Sequencer Study

What a full sequencer has to do for us, how eight other FM-1 firmwares do it, and the six choices that are yours before the mock-up.

In short. Melodee is the closest prior art to what you described: it keeps what you play as played, records live in patterns and arranges them into a song. It also records knob moves into a pattern, and it can keep what you just played even if REC was off. SLOOP has the best live song gestures: play sections on the next bar, chain them, and record the song while you play it. Felucca has the deepest step editing.

My recommendation:

  • Keep our "as played" notes.
  • Add patterns per track and a song of sections, which you can arrange by hand (Melodee's grid) or by playing (SLOOP).
  • Add motion recorded from any page while REC is on.
  • Add a steps view where you can draw notes, chords and hits.

The one thing that can't wait is somewhere to keep songs. Today a song is lost at power-off.

What stands out

  • Everyone separates patterns from the song. A pattern is one track's loop. The song says which pattern each track plays in each section. SLOOP alone saves all four tracks together as a section (A–D), which is simpler but means you can't reuse one drum pattern under two different chord parts.
  • "As played" is now common. Melodee keeps recorded notes with their timing beside the steps. Felucca, SLOOP and OberVave store the offset from the grid as a nudge. Our notes-as-played model is already right; it needs a steps view on top.
  • Erasing live is the same everywhere: hold EDIT and a key while the playhead passes, as on the MPC. Ours matches it.
  • Motion is recorded as you turn, while REC is on (Melodee, OberVave), and edited afterwards as lanes (Melodee's MOTION, Felucca's AUTOMATION list). Every firmware caps it with a shared pool of events (64 in Melodee, 128 in Felucca).
  • The live song gestures are SLOOP's: pick a section and it starts on the next bar, chain a few, and "record the song as you play it". Melodee does the same quietly: while you jam, each loop is logged, and one hold turns the log into the song.
  • Storage decides the size. Melodee shares 1,024 timed notes across a whole project. The FM-1's flash allows a few songs, not dozens, without touching the other firmwares' areas. That is a low-level question for me to settle from prior art (CLAUDE.md), and I'll tell you what I decide.

4 · Choices that are yours

These change what a child or a producer sees, hears or keeps, so they wait for you. Where I suggest an option, its box is outlined.

A · What a section holds

Suggested

A pattern per track

Each section picks a pattern, or a rest, for each track (Melodee, Felucca, Tessera).
  • The same drums under a new chorus
  • A track can rest
  • A grid of 4 tracks × sections to show

The whole set

A section is all four tracks at once, A–D (SLOOP).
  • Simplest: save the loop as B
  • Reusing a part means copying it

B · How a song is made

Suggested

Both, playing first

Play sections live and the order is kept (SLOOP, Melodee's jam log), then fix it on the grid by hand.
  • Feels like playing, not data entry
  • Two ways to learn

Only by hand

The SONG grid alone (Felucca).
  • One way, easy to explain
  • Slower, and less fun on a small screen

C · How many and how long

Suggested

8 patterns a track, 16 sections

Patterns of 1–8 bars, as today. 16 sections, each with repeats. About 4 saved songs.
  • Fits a short song for a child or a full track
  • The final numbers follow the flash we can use

16 patterns, 64 sections

Melodee's size.
  • Room for long songs
  • Fewer saved songs; more to scroll

D · Motion

Suggested

Every knob, from any page, while REC

Includes octave, mod, PERFORM pairs and FX. One pool per song. A MOTION view to see and clear lanes.
  • What you asked for
  • A full pool needs a clear message

A chosen few

Only cutoff, mod and octave.
  • Smaller, simpler
  • Less expressive

E · Drawing steps

Suggested

Keys are the steps

On STEPS the 16 white keys are 16 steps: tap one to set it or clear it. On drums ALGORITHM picks the sound. On other tracks a step takes the key or chord held. Your played notes stay as played; drawn ones sit on the grid.
  • Hands-on, like a drum machine
  • The keys stop playing notes on that view

Cursor and knobs only

A piano roll edited with K1–K4.
  • The keys keep playing
  • Slower to draw a beat

F · Keep what I just played

Suggested

Yes

REC held while not recording keeps the last bars.
  • Nothing good is lost, even without REC
  • REC held has to mean only this

Not now

Later, if it is missed.
  • One thing less to build
