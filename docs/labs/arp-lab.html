Arp Lab

A first concept for ARP: one button for every track, which turns what you hold into a pattern in time. On DRUMS it becomes note repeat. Press Start sound, tap ARP to switch it on, then hold some keys.

ARP tapped: on and off (its light shows it). ARP double-tapped: LATCH, so the pattern keeps going after you let go, until you hold new keys. SELECT: the rhythm. ALGORITHM: the track.

What ARP is

  • One ARP button for every track (the owner's idea): tap it on or off, double-tap for latch.
  • It plays what you hold in time with the song: notes on MELODY and BASS, the chord on CHORDS (a pad of one finger arpeggiates the whole chord), a sound on DRUMS (note repeat).
  • REC records the notes it plays, and they land in the sequence on the top line.

The knobs (ARP page)

  • K1 MODE: up, down, up-down, down-up, as played, random, converge, diverge, pinky (the top note between every note), thumb (the bottom note between), chord (all at once, in rhythm).
  • K2 RATE: 1/4, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32, following the tempo and swing.
  • K3 OCTAVES: 1 to 4. K4 GATE: short to legato.
  • SELECT RHYTHM: straight, or a rhythm with rests and accents (offbeat, 3-3-2, skip, gallop, reggaeton, dotted).

On DRUMS: note repeat

  • Hold a sound and it repeats at RATE: hat rolls, snare rolls, trap rolls.
  • K1 becomes RAMP: flat, crescendo or fade (the repeats get louder or softer while you hold).
  • Two sounds held: they alternate, as on an MPC.

From the other firmwares

  • Felucca: the most modes (up, down, up-down, random, as played, repeat, converge, diverge, pinky, thumb, walk, chord) and latch. Its ARP is a page plus a layer.
  • OberVave: 1–4 octaves, 1/4 to 1/32 with triplets, gate, latch (HOLD), and arps kept with the sound.
  • SLOOP: ARP held is note repeat (a roll), recorded as ratchets.
  • Ours adds: rhythms with rests and accents, the chord pad feeding it, and note repeat with a ramp on drums.
