Beat guide: lessons 1–9, and practice

A sketch of the guided mode for grown-ups who never made a beat, and of how it hands you over to the firmware. Lesson 1 takes a song apart with the mixer: tracks, drums, styles, tempo, key. Lesson 2 finds the tempo with your hands, then builds a beat on it. Lesson 3 is groove: shape a stiff beat into a human one with the knobs. Lesson 4 is keys and chords: why some notes sound good together and others don't, how a key and a progression are chosen. Lesson 5 is chord colours: 7, 9, sus and more, voicings, strum and arp. Lesson 6 is the bass, lesson 7 the melody, each with a song of its own. Lesson 8 tries three ways to start a song; lesson 9 records your own. Then two practice games: scales and chords, with the lights, for stars.

Try it: follow the box above: it says what to do, the control to use glows, and a ✓ shows when you did it; then NEXT › (SELECT › on the FM-1). The keys: tap them, or the computer keys A S D F G H J K L (white, from C4) and W E T Y U O P (black); the drums are always the three lit keys side by side, KICK, SNARE, HAT (also Z X C), and for tapping the tempo any key (or the space bar) will do.
The controls keep one meaning everywhere: K1–K4 the mixer (drums, bass, chords, melody), ALGORITHM the tempo, PRESETS the drums and the style, OCT− / OCT+ the key, SELECT the step, HOME (a tap) the list of lessons. in lesson 3 K1–K4 and PRESETS shape the groove instead (the screen shows them); REC records drums, from lesson 2; EDIT held with a drum key erases it (Shift on a computer keyboard), as in the Producer mode. Show me and Clear drums stand in for a held button on the FM-1.
The song is our own: two chords, Am7 and Fmaj7, on a Wurlitzer electric piano, one per bar, struck again just before the bar ends (in the last bar that hit is already the next chord: a push); a bass guitar on two notes an octave apart, and a note that leads into the next chord; a sparse hook on a harp, with an echo; real drums with ghost notes, a little swing and human timing. Your own MIDI can replace it.
The sounds are recordings: the hip-hop kick, snare and hat are the owner's own; the other drums from Sample Pi (the Sonic Pi samples, CC0; on a drum bus with a little saturation), the bass guitar by D. Smolken / Karoryfer (CC0), the harp from the Versilian Community Sample Library (CC0), the Wurlitzer EP200 by Greg Sullivan (CC BY 3.0), all as collected at github.com/danigb/samples. On the FM-1 the sounds would be its own engines and drum kits, so this lab sounds better than the device will.
